How I evaluate AI

The sequence I follow on a response. Select a step.

    1. Understand the prompt. Identify what the user actually wants, including implicit constraints and context. Intent and audience. Explicit and implied constraints.
    2. Evaluate the response. Read the whole answer before judging any part. Completeness and clarity. Tone fit.
    3. Check instruction following. Compare the output against every requirement in the prompt. Format, length, scope. Anything ignored or added.
    4. Check factuality. Verify claims independently rather than trusting fluency. Hallucinations. Unsupported claims and misinformation.
    5. Check reasoning. Follow the logic step by step. Fallacies and gaps. Conclusions that do not follow.
    6. Check safety and policy. Review against content and policy guidelines. Harmful or policy-violating content. Bias and ethical risk.
    7. Compare alternatives. Rank side by side against the rubric, not by which sounds better. Pairwise preference. Mark ambiguity as unsure where protocol says so.
    8. Write the justification. State the reason for the ranking so it can be audited and calibrated. Specific evidence. Consistent with the guidelines.
    9. Final quality decision. Submit the score or ranking and feed recurring weaknesses back. Decision recorded. Patterns reported for improvement.

    Flagship case study: ranking two LLM answers

    Task
    Prompt: "In under 120 words, explain to a general reader how vaccines train the immune system." Rank two responses against a standard rubric.
    Response A

    Clear, within the limit, well structured. States that vaccines give "100% protection."

    Response B

    Within the limit. Explains how antigens prime memory cells and says protection is not absolute. Does not mention boosters.

    CriterionABNote
    Instruction followingStrongStrongBoth meet the length limit and audience.
    CompletenessStrongModerateB omits boosters.
    FactualityWeakStrongA: "100% protection" is unsupported.
    ReasoningModerateStrongA: right mechanism, overreaching conclusion.
    SafetyModerateStrongOverstated medical certainty can mislead.
    OverallRejectPreferred
    Written justification
    B preferred. A overstates efficacy with no support, a factual and safety problem in a health answer. B is accurate and safe; the missing booster detail is minor and easy to fix.
    Final quality decision
    A rejected for an unsupported claim. B accepted as preferred, with the booster gap noted as a minor completeness issue.
    Why it matters
    Preference data that rewards polish over accuracy can teach models to sound sure rather than be right. The review exists to catch one confident error behind fluent writing.
